# Community and the news edition

- `/communaute/`: “Pour vous” alternates news and social publications, with a citizen contribution slot every five items when available. Within news and social pools, source ranks rotate through media outlets and account/platform pairs to prevent one prolific source from dominating. Mixing occurs before pagination so unchanged data produces stable pages without duplicates. Dedicated type filters remain chronological. Topic and publication-type filters let readers focus the feed. Public discussions and likes are stored in SQL. The government directory remains at `/repertoire/`.
- `/actualites/`: latest approved lead story, three concise headlines, and a 3 × 3 grid of nine recent stories per page. Date takes precedence over media preference, so the lead updates when a newer approved story arrives. Manual refresh and the existing periodic refresh retain the active filters. Reading, source links, and existing AI conversation history remain available. Both News and Community use shared yellow and restrained red accents, with blue remaining primary and separate dark-mode contrast colors.

Apply the schema with `python manage.py migrate`. No extra dependencies or API credentials are needed for community publishing. AI uses the configured local Ollama URL and model.

On a Community page visit, a background task generates up to three short news summaries, at most every ten minutes per web process. It first attempts to read the original publication page and saves the article text. Long articles are processed in passages before the final summary so the end is not silently discarded. If the publisher is inaccessible, the saved text is used and the card explicitly says so. Summaries are one paragraph of one to three sentences, requested at 80 words and rejected above 100 words. They are explicitly labeled as AI, use exactly the original news image URL, retain the original source, and link to `/actualites/?article=<id>` to open that specific story. The mixed feed represents each story once, using the AI paragraph when ready; pending stories retain their existing excerpt. The AI filter also exposes the summaries with the same source image and article link. Set `COMMUNITY_AUTO_BRIEFS=false` to disable generation. Generate more deliberately with `python manage.py generate_community_posts --limit 3`; a scheduler can run the command while the website is closed. Failed generation creates no placeholder post.

Readers can share links from X/Twitter, YouTube, LinkedIn, Instagram, Bluesky, and Threads. Facebook and TikTok are excluded from publication, the feed, direct discussion access, and automatic discovery. Existing imported posts from excluded platforms are hidden rather than deleted. Platform labels come from the URL host. External links are never fetched by the community publication endpoint.

With `TAVILY_API_KEY` configured on the server, visiting Community or refreshing the first feed page starts background discovery of public social posts indexed by Tavily. Three searches target exact selected X account handles, grouped into media, journalists/civil-society voices, and institutions, for DRC posts from the last week. The maintained catalogue in `apps/web_ui/social_accounts.py` retains identity references and a review date; it is an editorial selection, not a follower ranking or verification badge. Examples include Radio Okapi, ACTUALITE.CD, TOP CONGO FM, Stanis Bujakera, Daniel Michombero, Denis Mukwege, the Presidency and MONUSCO. Search hits must match a selected account in their original URL; mentioning a famous name is insufficient. Automatic imports currently focus on these X accounts. Other permitted platforms remain available for manually shared links.

The normalizer isolates the quoted original post from scraped page titles, repeated text, timestamps, counters, and other users’ replies. It unescapes Markdown and adds paragraph breaks without generating new facts. Ambiguous unclosed quotations are rejected. Source attribution and the original link stay visible. Raw titles, raw excerpts, original URLs and normalization details are preserved in `source_metadata` for audit and reprocessing. `python manage.py fetch_social_posts --repair-only` cleans old imports without a Tavily call, preserving comments and reactions; excluded, unselected, or unclear imports are hidden. Canonical URLs and a unique hash prevent duplicate imports. Hidden posts are never automatically made visible again. Imported posts show “Repéré via Tavily”; the displayed date is discovery time, not an asserted publication time. Excerpts are not verified or guaranteed complete. This is indexed-web discovery, not a complete real-time social API feed.

News and Community share `source-brand.js`: source labels include a favicon, with a persistent monogram fallback when both the original favicon and the secondary favicon service fail. This applies to lead stories, briefs, timeline rows, social cards, source previews and article/discussion source links.

X photos are fetched on a best-effort basis from X's public embed metadata. The response must match the original post ID and selected account handle. Only original photo entries on `pbs.twimg.com/media/` are kept; avatars, quoted posts, replies and unrelated hosts are ignored. Up to four photo URLs are stored, with the first shown full width and linked to the original post. Photos are cached, failed requests do not remove existing images, and no placeholder photograph is invented. The public embed endpoint may change or be unavailable. Backfill existing posts with `python manage.py fetch_social_posts --images-only`; no Tavily searches are made by this option.

Instagram discovery targets `@radiookapi` and `@actualite.cd`, whose public profile identities were checked during implementation. A fourth search rotates through these accounts. A result must be a publication permalink and include explicit publisher attribution in its indexed metadata (for example a platform byline or likes/comments author/date prefix); a casual account mention is insufficient. Publisher metadata is stripped from the displayed caption. A selected media account is not a guarantee of factual accuracy. Profiles, login pages, ambiguous or unattributed captions are skipped. Instagram can still be shared manually with its original link. Facebook and TikTok remain excluded.

The default refresh interval is 900 seconds per web process, configurable via `COMMUNITY_SOCIAL_REFRESH_SECONDS` (minimum 300). Each refresh makes six bounded searches, rotating through the catalogues across refreshes. Disable with `COMMUNITY_SOCIAL_DISCOVERY=false`. Requests use the existing Tavily account and consume search credits. Existing posts remain visible if Tavily fails. The feed reports refreshing, unavailable, partially successful, and missing-key states without exposing credentials. Run `python manage.py fetch_social_posts` for an immediate import or from a scheduler when the page is closed. The UI checks for newly imported posts briefly while discovery is in progress.

The six search groups are media, journalists/civil society, institutions, public figures, DRC embassies, and Instagram. Embassy targets include France, Belgium, the United States and the United Kingdom. Additional public voices include Julienne Lusenge, Martin Fayulu, Moïse Katumbi and Patrick Muyaya. Their posts remain attributed to the author; the selection is not endorsement. `fetch_social_posts --rounds 3` runs three catalogue rotations for an initial backfill (maximum four rounds).

RFI coverage is restricted to stories with an identifiable DRC connection in their title, summary, or main article text. Generic references to Congo-Brazzaville are insufficient. The same policy is enforced at RSS import, news lists/counts/direct article access, community feeds, and AI summary generation. `OfficialNews.objects.approved()` is the shared read policy. `python manage.py enforce_news_policy` unapproves old unrelated RFI stories without deleting their data; linked AI briefs are also hidden.

Older pictures: `fetch_social_posts --images-only --force-images --limit 100` retries missing X images oldest first, bypassing cached failures and skipping already illustrated posts. It also accepts the original post's own article-card image on `pbs.twimg.com/card_img/` when there is no attached photo. It never reads cards from quoted posts or avatars. `python manage.py backfill_news_images --limit 20` retries original publisher images for approved news missing a picture. Posts without a recoverable source picture stay without one.

Full X content: `fetch_social_posts --content-only --force-images --limit 100` retries both full text and images for older posts, including illustrated posts whose text is still an excerpt. The original author's native text is used, preferring `note_tweet` long-form text; explicitly truncated responses do not replace a longer stored post. Full native text has no 3,000-character storage cutoff and is returned unabridged by the feed. X text is displayed expanded, without the four-line clamp. Other users' replies and quoted-post text are not silently merged into the author's message. Successfully recovered text is marked in metadata and labeled as the original X post; unavailable source text stays labeled as an indexed excerpt. Reprocessing old imports preserves recovered native text.

Image lookup also traverses the original post's own rich body and long-form media entries. If no photo or card is found, links in that post's own URL entities can be followed to selected public publisher domains. Inline and lazy-loaded images are read from the main article body, including images appearing after several paragraphs; sidebars, navigation and logos are excluded. Redirects outside the selected domains are rejected. Linked-article image provenance is stored and displayed as a separate credit link. No arbitrary external link is fetched and no unrelated replacement picture is generated.

Posting uses an anonymous browser session and a user-chosen display name, not a verified identity. Likes are unique per browser session. Names and posts are public. Administrative `is_visible` fields allow staff to hide posts and comments through Django admin. AI authorship is a server-controlled flag; ordinary posts cannot set it. CSRF protection and per-session process-local write limits are enabled; these are not a substitute for account-based quotas or distributed abuse controls for a public deployment.

AI questions are saved as comments before requesting the model. If Ollama fails, the question remains and the UI offers a retry. Replies use the selected source, its stored text, and the latest visible comments; they do not claim live web verification. The article reader continues to use the existing web-assisted article chat.

Run `python manage.py test apps.web_ui.test_community apps.web_ui.test_news_sources` for the feed, persistence, filters, likes, reply isolation, CSRF, AI failure and source grounding, briefing deduplication, and news pagination checks.
